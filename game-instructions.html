How to Run Every Game

← The Game Room

Choose a game, open its instructions, and read the setup aloud. The host keeps the round moving; players explain their reasoning before answers are revealed.

Areopagus games

Theology Bingo

Private cards · host-led clues · Discord voice

Setup: Use the launcher to choose an edition, winning pattern, and fresh game code. The host keeps the dashboard and caller sheet private and sends each player one numbered card link.
Host
  1. Open the host dashboard and question presenter; announce the winning pattern.
  2. Read one clue at a time without saying the answer, then confirm the term and add it to the called list.
  3. When someone calls Bingo, ask for their card number and verify it against the called terms.
  4. Pause for questions between clues; never expose private card links or the answer key.
Players
  1. Open only your assigned private card and keep Discord voice available.
  2. Identify each clue; mark the matching square only after the host confirms the term.
  3. Call “Bingo” when your announced pattern is complete and give the host your card number.
  4. Do not share card links or mark terms that have not been called.

Creed Memory

Learn · fill · sequence · quiz · recitation

Setup: Choose one creed, agree on the Apostles’ descent wording or Nicene Filioque setting, then choose a study mode. Device progress belongs to the device being used.
Host or study leader
  1. Select the creed, wording variant, and one mode for the round.
  2. In Learn mode, assign sections to readers; in Fill or Quiz, require an answer before checking.
  3. For Sequence, let players agree before moving a section. For Recitation, reveal only after the speaker finishes.
  4. Rotate speakers and end with one explanation of why the wording matters.
Players
  1. Read the selected section aloud and attend to exact wording.
  2. Give one answer before using reveal or check controls.
  3. In group recitation, do not prompt unless the speaker asks.
  4. Use the explanation to connect memorized words to the doctrine being confessed.

Bible Who Am I?

Identify the person, passage, and narrative significance

Setup: Open a prompt and have one player answer first. The round is complete only when the group names the figure, locates the passage, and explains why the clue matters.
Host
  1. Read the full clue once and choose the first respondent.
  2. If needed, use “Ask the room”: one helper names the book or period and another gives one extra clue.
  3. Ask for the passage and narrative significance before revealing.
  4. Reveal the answer, clarify it, then load a new prompt.
Players
  1. Answer without searching on the first attempt.
  2. Name the biblical figure, likely passage, and the clue’s importance.
  3. Helpers must not say the answer directly.
  4. After reveal, correct the answer in your own words.

Who Said It?

Identify the source and the controversy behind the quotation

Setup: Read the quotation without its attribution. A complete answer names the theologian, creed, confession, or catechism and explains the issue it addresses.
Host
  1. Read the quotation and invite one identification.
  2. Allow only one hint category: century, tradition, document type, or controversy.
  3. Ask the player to explain the historical or doctrinal setting.
  4. Reveal and supply the source context.
Players
  1. Name the most precise source you can.
  2. Explain which controversy or doctrine makes the line recognizable.
  3. If giving a hint, use only the category the host approved.
  4. Compare your reasoning with the revealed context.

Bible & Church History Trivia

Advanced multiple choice with required reasoning

Setup: Select a question, but do not click an answer immediately. One player answers and another must eliminate one alternative with a reason.
Host
  1. Read the question and all four choices.
  2. Ask one player for an answer and another to eliminate a distractor.
  3. Require a reason before selecting the choice.
  4. Read the explanation whether the answer was right or wrong.
Players
  1. Choose from knowledge rather than guessing quickly.
  2. Connect the answer to a text, date, council, or doctrinal distinction.
  3. Challenge reasoning respectfully before the click.
  4. Keep the score secondary to learning the explanation.

Scripture Scavenger Hunt

Build a contextual two-text case

Setup: Draw a claim. The group must find two passages that work together, read enough context, and answer one plausible counter-reading.
Host
  1. Read the claim and set a reasonable search time.
  2. Assign a primary passage, a confirming or balancing passage, and a contextual objection.
  3. Ask what each passage adds that the other does not.
  4. Reveal the host examples only after the argument is complete.
Players
  1. Search Scripture and read the surrounding paragraph.
  2. Explain the actual contribution of your passage.
  3. Offer a fair counter-reading rather than a weak objection.
  4. Combine the texts into one coherent answer.

Steelman Challenge

Present a disputed position as an informed advocate would

Setup: Draw a position. The group must state its strongest positive case, answer its best obvious objection, and make the result recognizable to an informed advocate.
Host
  1. Assign the positive case, response to objection, and fairness check to different players.
  2. Stop caricatures and ask for primary biblical or historical support.
  3. Let the fairness checker request one revision.
  4. Reveal the host guide and compare it to the group’s version.
Players
  1. Argue the assigned position rather than your own preferred view.
  2. Use its strongest sources and distinctions.
  3. Answer the strongest likely objection directly.
  4. Evaluate only after the position has been stated fairly.

Steelman Challenge: Heresy Mode

Expose the controlling premise, then give an orthodox refutation

Setup: This is advanced historical-theology practice, not advocacy. State the condemned claim accurately, identify why it once persuaded people, then refute it from orthodox sources.
Host
  1. Assign one player the strongest supporting text, one the hidden premise, and one the decisive orthodox distinction.
  2. Require historically recognizable wording; reject invented extremes.
  3. Do not reveal until all three contributions are heard.
  4. Read the analysis and key sources, then restate the orthodox correction.
Players
  1. Describe the error accurately without promoting it.
  2. Separate a true concern from the false conclusion.
  3. Name the premise that drives the mistake.
  4. Refute it with the precise orthodox distinction and relevant sources.

Explain It Simply

Teach an advanced doctrine in one clear minute

Setup: Draw a doctrine and choose one speaker. The goal is clarity for a thoughtful new Christian without losing the distinction that makes the doctrine necessary.
Host
  1. Give the speaker one minute.
  2. Ask another player for a biblical text and another for a possible analogy.
  3. Require the speaker to use the text and to accept, revise, or reject the analogy.
  4. Reveal the guide and name one thing the explanation preserved well.
Players
  1. Avoid undefined technical vocabulary.
  2. State the core claim, its biblical basis, and why it matters.
  3. Use analogies cautiously; say where they break down.
  4. Ask one sincere beginner’s question afterward.

Church History Timeline

Order closely related events and justify placements

Setup: Draw a timeline and tap the events from earliest to latest. Every placement should be supported by a date, ruler, controversy, or neighboring event.
Host
  1. Read every event before anyone moves.
  2. Invite players in rotation to place one event and justify it.
  3. Allow discussion and one revision before checking.
  4. Reveal the correct order and read why the sequence matters.
Players
  1. Place only one event per turn.
  2. Give evidence for its position rather than only a guess.
  3. Use relationships between events when exact dates are unknown.
  4. After checking, explain the hardest neighboring pair.

Two Truths and an Error

Detect a subtle mistake and formulate the correction

Setup: Read all three statements. The error may be historically or exegetically subtle, so the group must defend the two truths as well as identify the false statement.
Host
  1. Assign one statement to each of three players.
  2. Require each player to defend or challenge it from a primary source.
  3. Take the group vote, then ask a fourth player for a precise correction.
  4. Select an answer and read the source trail.
Players
  1. Test your assigned statement before voting.
  2. Distinguish a minor wording problem from the actual error.
  3. Defend both true statements positively.
  4. Correct the error without replacing it with another overstatement.

Finish the Verse

Choose the exact continuation from real biblical phrasing

Setup: Read the reference and incomplete verse. All distractors sound scriptural, so use the paragraph’s argument and immediate context rather than rhythm alone.
Host
  1. Read the reference, prompt, and all choices.
  2. Ask one player for the paragraph’s argument and another to eliminate one choice by context.
  3. Do not let helpers quote the missing words.
  4. Select the answer and read the explanation and reference.
Players
  1. Recall the passage in context, not only as an isolated phrase.
  2. Explain why one plausible option belongs somewhere else.
  3. Choose the exact continuation.
  4. After reveal, read the surrounding passage aloud when practical.

Christian Would You Rather?

Reason through a real pastoral or theological tradeoff

Setup: Read both choices. Everyone chooses provisionally, then the main speaker must make the strongest case for the option they rejected.
Host
  1. Read the dilemma without signaling your preference.
  2. Invite a first choice and supporting reason.
  3. Assign another player to advocate for the unchosen option.
  4. Use the host follow-up, then allow a final revised answer.
Players
  1. Choose and name the value your choice protects.
  2. State the cost or weakness of your own choice.
  3. Summarize the opposing case fairly before responding.
  4. Change your answer only after explaining what persuaded you.

Deep Conversation Wheel

Exegete a curated theological question across several texts

Setup: Choose a category or surprise topic. Read every listed passage, then answer the three study questions and test the conclusion against the strongest competing interpretation.
Host
  1. Spin once and assign the listed passages among readers.
  2. Keep each text in its literary and covenantal context.
  3. Assign one player the strongest competing interpretation and another the text it explains best.
  4. Require the first speaker to answer both before concluding.
Players
  1. Read your assigned passage aloud or summarize its immediate context.
  2. State what the passage actually affirms before synthesizing.
  3. Present the best counter-reading fairly.
  4. Prefer the conclusion that explains the complete set of texts.

External community-night games

Codenames in Discord

Free Discord Activity · team word association

Setup: Join one Discord voice channel, open Activities, launch Codenames, and have everyone join the Activity. Divide into two teams and choose one clue-giver for each.
Host
  1. Launch the Activity and confirm every player can see the board.
  2. Assign teams and clue-givers; explain the one-word clue plus number format.
  3. Remind clue-givers not to use visible board words, gestures, spelling, or extra hints.
  4. Keep turns moving and use the Activity’s result as final.
Players
  1. Join your assigned team inside the Activity.
  2. Clue-givers privately study the key and give only a legal one-word clue and number.
  3. Guessers may discuss openly, but the clue-giver must remain neutral.
  4. Stop when the team chooses to end its guesses or the turn ends.

Ticket to Ride

2–5 players · browser, app, or Switch

Setup: Agree on Board Game Arena or the official app, create a private table, invite 2–5 players, and keep the Areopagus Discord voice channel open for conversation.
Host
  1. Choose the platform and map before sending invitations.
  2. Explain the turn choices: draw train cards, claim a route, or draw destination tickets.
  3. Explain route points, completed tickets, penalties for incomplete tickets, and the end trigger.
  4. Start only after everyone has joined and understands where legal moves appear.
Players
  1. Join the private table using the platform account or invitation.
  2. Keep destination tickets private and take only the actions allowed by the interface.
  3. Use Discord voice socially, but do not disclose hidden cards or tickets.
  4. Let the app calculate final scoring and congratulate the winner.

Jackbox

Host-owned party pack · phones as controllers

Setup: The host launches an owned Party Pack and screen-shares it with sound in Discord. Players open jackbox.tv on a phone or browser and enter the room code and display name.
Host
  1. Choose a game that supports the number of attendees.
  2. Share the game window and audio, then show the room code only to the group.
  3. Confirm players can see the shared screen and submit from their devices.
  4. Use family-friendly settings and moderation controls appropriate for the server.
Players
  1. Watch the host’s Discord stream and keep jackbox.tv open separately.
  2. Enter the exact room code and a recognizable display name.
  3. Submit answers before the timer ends and follow the selected game’s prompts.
  4. Keep custom responses consistent with Areopagus community standards.

Blood on the Clocktower

Large-group social deduction · Storyteller required

Setup: Schedule the session, appoint an experienced Storyteller, choose a beginner-friendly script, and arrange a private way to distribute roles and receive night actions.
Storyteller
  1. Prepare the script and legal character distribution before the session.
  2. Explain good and evil teams, day/night phases, nominations, voting, death, and dead votes.
  3. Send roles privately and run night actions in the correct order.
  4. Remain neutral, give only legal information, track game state, and announce the result.
Players
  1. Keep your role and private information secret unless strategy calls for revealing it.
  2. During the day, share claims, question inconsistencies, nominate, and vote respectfully.
  3. During the night, communicate only when and how the Storyteller directs.
  4. Dead players remain involved; use the dead vote carefully and do not leave early.
